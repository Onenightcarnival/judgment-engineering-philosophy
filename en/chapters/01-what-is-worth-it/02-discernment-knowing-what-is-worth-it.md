# Discernment: Knowing What Is Worth It

Discernment is knowing what is worth it.

## Three outputs

| Output | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| What to choose | Pointing at the one option among several, at a glance |
| What not to choose | Ruling out the rest without trying each |
| What not to look at | Recognizing a question not worth thinking about, and spending no time on it |

Most of what discernment produces is a "no".

## Where it acts

Discernment acts one level above how the code is written: which tool, which paradigm, which layer, how tight the constraints.

See [Chapter 5](../05-discernment-sits-one-level-above-the-rules/00-overview.md).

## What its absence looks like

- Unable to tell good from bad.
- Copying the only thing ever seen.
- The acceptance standard shrinks to "it runs".

## Discernment can be learned

The path is guides plus practice. The condition is reading and practicing on one's own initiative.
