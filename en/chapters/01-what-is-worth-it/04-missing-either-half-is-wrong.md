# Missing Either Half Is Wrong

Judgment is discernment and discipline present at once.

## Four cells

| | With discipline | Without discipline |
|---|---|---|
| With discernment | Judgment | Sees it right, cannot do it |
| Without discernment | Does it, in the wrong direction | Drifts |

## Discernment without discipline

- Knows the refactoring is due; never starts.
- Knows the requirement is not worth building; takes it on.
- Sees the problem in review; lets it through.

The outcome is the same as having no discernment.

## Discipline without discernment

- The process is strict.
- The standards are complete.
- Every metric is green.
- The thing built was not worth building.

The more effort, the more waste.

## For agents

| | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Discernment without discipline | Starts well, falls back to the default as the context grows |
| Discipline without discernment | Follows instructions strictly and delivers the average |

## The order

Discipline first, then discernment.

1. Defer to the baseline and do as it says.
2. Understand why the baseline holds.
3. Improve on it.

## Unity

When both are present and no longer take effort, it is called taste. See the [final chapter](../08-unity-of-knowing-and-doing/00-overview.md).
