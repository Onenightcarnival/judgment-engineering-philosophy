# At the Level of Knowledge, Everyone Is Right

## Four levels

| Level | The question it answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Data | What happened | The endpoint responds in 800 ms on average |
| Information | What that means | Twice as slow as the previous version |
| Knowledge | How to do it | A cache would bring it down; so would rewriting the query |
| Wisdom | What is worth doing | Depends on how long this endpoint will live |

Judgment sits at the fourth level.

## An argument at the level of knowledge has no loser

| One side | The other side |
|---|---|
| A cache is the fastest fix | A cache brings consistency problems |
| Ship first, then iterate | Get the architecture right first |
| Use the mature framework | The framework is too heavy; writing our own fits better |

Every cell is correct knowledge. Both sides can produce evidence.

At the level of knowledge, this argument does not end.

## Why it cannot be settled

Each side has assumed a horizon and has not said it out loud.

| Claim | The unstated horizon |
|---|---|
| Ship first, then iterate | This quarter |
| Get the architecture right first | Three years |

## Go up one dimension

Add time, and the question changes from "who is right" to "right over which horizon".

What separates knowledge from wisdom is this one dimension.

Someone who has lived through a full cycle carries that dimension with them.
