# Three Sentences Are Enough

When the receiver is close enough, the principles take only a few sentences.

```text
One style for code, documents and comments

1. Declarative.
2. No self-explanation; present only the design result.
3. Let structure make the logic visible at a glance.
```

## Effect

| | Without the three sentences | With the three sentences |
|---|---|---|
| The deliverable | Wordy, self-explaining | Clean |

## How the length changes

| Model capability | The instruction needed |
|---|---|
| Weak | A long list of rules, a dedicated process for stripping boilerplate |
| Strong | A few principles; the rest is small fixes |

The length of an instruction is inversely proportional to the capability of its receiver.

## Three conditions for being short

- The writer has seen clearly. Compression is a product of discernment.
- The receiver is close enough.
- Everything else has already been handed to other carriers.

| Carrier | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Rules | What can be stated |
| Examples | What cannot be stated but can be made |
| Principles | Direction |

## The second sentence

The deliverable contains no reasoning process, no self-assessment, no "why it is written this way".

The explaining is done by the structure.

## One document

Design and architecture principles are written in one place, for people and for agents.

A document that is clear to both can be written in only one way.
