# Take the Mechanism Apart

Discernment starts with taking the mechanism apart.

A mechanism is a system's rules, numbers and interactions. Someone who knows the mechanism can predict the system's behavior without waiting for it to happen.

## Three things to take apart

| What | The question to answer | What it looks like in engineering |
|---|---|---|
| Rules | What it allows and what it forbids | Lifecycle, call order, who holds the state |
| Numbers | How large the cost, how large the gain | Milliseconds, tokens and money per call |
| Interactions | What it clashes with | Which combinations break, and in which settings it is the wrong choice |

Numbers are to be worked out. "Probably fast" is not a number.

## Afterwards

The dominant approach surfaces on its own. No discussion is needed.

Understanding of the mechanism makes up for weak execution. The reverse does not hold.

## What not taking it apart looks like

| | Mechanism taken apart | Not taken apart |
|---|---|---|
| The system appears as | A set of rules that can be reasoned through | A black box |
| Choices are made by | Reasoning | Trying |
| The reason given | "This will necessarily lead to that" | "It worked last time" |
| After the environment changes | Reason again | Try again |

## For agents

- Have the agent read the source before acting, and its output rests on the mechanism. Let it act directly, and its output rests on impressions.
- Whoever uses an agent has one more mechanism to take apart: the model itself. How it was trained decides what it delivers by default.
