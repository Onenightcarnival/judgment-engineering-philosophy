# Guides Plus Practice

Discernment forms in two steps, in a fixed order: read the guides first, then practice for a long time.

## The two steps

| | Guides | Practice |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Mechanisms and dominant approaches, already worked out by others | Doing it by hand, for years |
| What it gives | The standard for good and bad | The standard turned into intuition |
| Time needed | Weeks | Years |
| With this step alone | Can tell good from bad, cannot produce it | Repeats existing habits |

## Guides come first

After reading a good guide, good and bad can be told apart at once. This step does not take years.

Practicing without having read the guides trains the mistakes in.

Guides have to be chosen. In any field, the guides that time has filtered number only a few.

## The form of practice

The most effective practice is gatekeeping: reviewing and refactoring what others hand in, over a long period.

Every review answers the same question: is this piece worth keeping. The number of answers piles up into discernment.

## This path has no pain

Discernment can be taught. The condition is that the person reads and practices on their own initiative.

Among people who read the same guides, some are transformed within half a year and some do not change. The difference is initiative, which no guide supplies.

## For agents

| | A person | An agent |
|---|---|---|
| Guides | Books | The principles and design documents written for it |
| Practice | Years of gatekeeping | Examples, plus one correction after another |
| Where the result of practice stays | In the person | In documents, rules and examples |

An agent's practice does not stay in the agent. Each correction has to land in a document, a rule or an example; otherwise the next time starts from zero.
