# People Cannot Teach People; Events Teach in One Lesson

## Two ways of teaching

| | Taught by people | Taught by events |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Explanations, reminders, review comments | The consequence lands on the person directly |
| What changes | Knowing | Behavior |
| Needs repeating | Yes, and to no effect | Once |

## Three conditions for an event

- The consequence is real.
- The consequence lands on the person who made the decision.
- The consequence arrives soon enough for that person to recognize which decision brought it.

Missing one, the event degrades into a principle. Watching someone else's incident still yields only "knowing".

## For people

The event that changes behavior has to be lived through. Nothing substitutes for it.

Living through it guarantees nothing either. Most people go through events and do not change.

## For agents

An agent feels no pain. Its events are checks that fail on the spot.

| Event | Lands on | How soon |
|---|---|---|
| A lint error | The turn that wrote the code | On the spot |
| A failing test | Same | On the spot |
| A failed build | Same | On the spot |

These events can be built in advance.

## For people, part of the event can be built too

Bring the arrival of the consequence forward.

| The consequence shows up | Can the decision behind it be recognized |
|---|---|
| At commit time | Yes |
| At review time | Mostly |
| A month after release | Mostly not |

The earlier the check, the more the event behaves like an event.
